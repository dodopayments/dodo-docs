## What Is Subscription Dunning?

Dunning automatically detects when a subscription enters a recoverable state and sends a sequence of emails prompting the customer to take action:

- **On Hold subscriptions**: Renewal payment failed (insufficient funds, expired card, etc.)
- **Cancelled subscriptions**: Customer cancelled from the customer portal

A **5% recovery fee** is charged on successfully recovered revenue (only when at least one dunning email was sent).

<Warning>
Only customer-initiated cancellations from the customer portal trigger the "cancelled" dunning sequence. Merchant-initiated cancellations, system cancellations (tax errors, business not live), and `cancel_at_next_billing_date` do not trigger dunning.
</Warning>

## How Dunning Works

<Steps>
<Step title="Trigger">
Dunning is triggered when a subscription enters one of two states:
- **On Hold**: A renewal payment failed (insufficient funds, expired card, etc.)
- **Cancelled**: The customer cancelled their subscription from the customer portal
</Step>

<Step title="Email Sequence">
Based on the trigger state, the system sends up to 4 dunning emails at configured intervals. Each email includes a link to the customer portal where the customer can update their payment method.
</Step>

<Step title="Recovery">
When the customer updates their payment method through the customer portal, a charge is automatically created for remaining dues. If the payment succeeds, the subscription is reactivated and the dunning attempt is marked as recovered.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Status Lifecycle

| Status | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| `recovering` | Dunning attempt is active; emails are being sent |
| `recovered` | Customer updated payment method and payment succeeded |
| `exhausted` | All emails sent with no recovery, or subscription state changed unexpectedly |

<Info>
When a dunning attempt is marked as `exhausted`, the subscription state is not modified. The subscription remains in its current state (on hold or cancelled).
</Info>

### Email Sequences

Dunning supports two email sequences — **On Hold** and **Cancelled** — each with up to 4 configurable emails.

<Frame caption="Dunning email sequence editor">
<img src="/images/recovery/dunning-email-config.png" alt="Dunning email configuration showing the on-hold and cancelled sequences with timing and content options" />
</Frame>

Each email has the following configurable fields:

| Setting | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Enabled** | Toggle individual emails on or off without deleting them |
| **Delay** | How long after the trigger event to send this email |
| **Subject** | Email subject line with variable placeholders |
| **Body** | HTML email body with variable placeholders |
| **Reply-To** | Email address for customer replies |

**Default email timing:**

| Sequence | Email # | Default Delay |
|----------|---------|---------------|
| On Hold | 1 | 10 days |
| On Hold | 2 | 15 days |
| On Hold | 3 | 20 days |
| On Hold | 4 | 25 days |
| Cancelled | 1 | 10 days |
| Cancelled | 2 | 15 days |
| Cancelled | 3 | 20 days |
| Cancelled | 4 | 25 days |

### Email Template Variables

| Placeholder | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| `{payment_form_link}` | Link to the customer portal payment method update page |
| `{subscription_id}` | Subscription identifier for reference |
| `{failure_reason}` | Human-readable payment failure reason |
| `{store_name}` | Your business name |
| `{store_owner_name}` | Your business owner name |
| `{store_contact_email}` | Your support email address |

<Info>
Dunning emails do not include an unsubscribe link. They are transactional notifications about an existing billing relationship, following industry standard practice (Stripe, Recurly follow the same convention).
</Info>

## Recovery Fee

Dunning charges a **5% recovery fee** on successfully recovered revenue. The fee is calculated on the total payment amount including taxes and other fees.

| Condition | Fee Charged? |
|-----------|-------------|
| Customer updates payment method and payment succeeds after at least 1 email | Yes (5%) |
| Customer updates payment method independently before any email is sent | No |

<Info>
Recovery fees appear as `dunning_fees` entries in your [Balance Ledger](/api-reference/balance-ledger/list-ledger-entries).
</Info>

## Edge Cases

| Scenario | Behavior |
|----------|----------|
| Customer updates payment method before any email | Recovery is detected but no recovery fee is charged (requires at least 1 email sent) |
| Customer purchases another subscription from same business | Dunning attempt is marked as exhausted |
| Subscription state changes unexpectedly | Worker checks state before each send; marks exhausted or recovered as appropriate |
| All dunning emails exhausted | Dunning attempt is marked as exhausted; subscription state is not modified |

## Best Practices

- **Start with defaults**: The default email timing is designed with reasonable spacing (10-25 days) to avoid overwhelming customers while maintaining urgency.
- **Personalize emails**: Include the failure reason (`{failure_reason}`) in on-hold emails so customers understand why their subscription was paused.
- **Test in test mode**: Dunning settings are configured separately for test and live modes. Test your email sequences before going live.
- **Monitor success rates**: Track which email in the sequence drives the most recoveries. If later emails have near-zero conversion, consider disabling them.
- **Coordinate with support**: Ensure your support team knows that dunning emails are being sent, so they can assist customers who reach out.
- **Review subscription states**: Pair dunning with `subscription.on_hold` and `subscription.cancelled` webhooks for comprehensive lifecycle tracking.

<Info>
Dunning works alongside the existing subscription on-hold and reactivation flows in the customer portal. Once enabled, it runs automatically with no additional integration required.
</Info>
